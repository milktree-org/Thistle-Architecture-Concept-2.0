## 66. The Decision and Recommended Roadmap, four studies

### Harpenden Police Station, Harpenden, Hertfordshire

Commercial to Residential. Options Tested. November 2025. Only The Decision
and the Roadmap are new; the Brief, What We Found and Our Recommendation
already on the page stand.

**The Decision.** The planning route mapped before the offer.

The appraisal confirmed that residential is a realistic future for the
building once the Policy SP7 test is met, and that reusing a thirty-year-old
building will score better with the council than knocking it down. The sketch
scheme shows four two-bedroom flats of 97 to 104 sqm over a retained
commercial ground floor with 58 sqm of shared amenity. The two things that
decide this deal sit outside the drawings: the restrictive covenant tying the
site to community uses, and evidence that the police function has been
re-provided elsewhere. The client went into negotiations knowing both, and in
what order to settle them.

Roadmap: 1 Covenant and title advice, including indemnity. 2 Policy SP7
evidence that the police use is re-provided. 3 Pre-application meeting with
St Albans. 4 Measured survey and condition check. 5 Full planning
application. 6 Technical design and construction.

### Axis House, Compton, Newbury

Commercial to Residential. Go. October 2025. Only The Decision and the
Roadmap are new. Note the stat and the text read two sketch proposals, not
three, per item 24.

**The Decision.** Two schemes tested, one taken forward.

The feasibility settled on the terrace: four three-bedroom houses of around
83 sqm each and one larger two-bedroom unit, working with the existing
structural grid and keeping the building within its footprint. The site's
protected landscape setting rules out Class MA prior approval, so full
planning was always the route, and the sketch testing meant the application
could be drawn straight from the preferred scheme. The application went in
in March 2026 and the project now sits under Projects as In planning.

Roadmap: 1 Measured survey. 2 Pre-application advice. 3 Full planning
application. 4 Technical design. 5 Tender. 6 Construction.

### Greyfriars, King's Lynn, Norfolk. Full rewrite of the card and the page

Mixed Use. Go. November 2025. The page currently describes a nine-bed option
and Options 2 and 4. The options drawn were a residential conversion of
eight flats and a seventeen-room en-suite HMO, both with the extension.
Everything below replaces what is on the card and the page.

**Index card.** One building, two viable futures. We drew a residential
conversion and a seventeen-room co-living HMO for the same envelope so the
client could compare income, build scope and planning risk directly.

Stats: Routes compared, 2. HMO option, 17 en suite.

**01 The Brief.** Greyfriars Chambers is a landmark corner building on St
James Street: a former public house, extended in an Art Deco style,
refurbished as offices in 2018 and occupied by an accountancy practice, with
around 365 sqm over two floors and parking to the rear. The client held it
under an option agreement and needed to know, before exercising it, whether
the building should become conventional flats or a large co-living HMO. Each
route carries a different planning risk, build cost and income, and
committing to the wrong one early would have meant abortive design fees and
a weaker planning story.

**02 What We Found.** We sketched the building both ways to the same
standard. Option 1 tests a residential conversion into eight flats, with an
extension. Option 2 tests a seventeen-bedroom en-suite HMO with the same
extension, with the shared kitchen, living and laundry distributed across the
plan rather than pushed into one corner, a plant room and a cleaner's store.
Both layouts are colour-coded by room function with every bedroom size stated
on the drawing, so the two schemes compare like for like. Room sizes on the
HMO option run from 10.8 to 22 sqm, well clear of the licensing minimums.

**03 Our Recommendation.** Go, on a phased planning strategy. Establish the
residential principle first through Class MA prior approval for the
office-to-residential conversion, which is a low-risk application with a
fixed timetable, then use that consent as the fallback while a full planning
application is made for the larger co-living scheme. The co-living scheme is
the higher-value outcome and the one the building suits best, but the client
should never be in a position where the only consent in hand is the harder
one.

**The Proposed Layout, caption.** Option 1 converts the offices into eight
flats with the extension. Option 2 takes seventeen en-suite bedrooms with
shared living, kitchen and laundry spread across both floors.

**The Decision.** A phased route that unlocks the bigger scheme.

The feasibility turned an abstract use-class question into a direct
comparison of two drawn schemes, and recommended securing the simpler consent
first so the larger one could be pursued from a position of strength. The
client exercised the option. Class MA prior approval has since been granted
and the co-living scheme is in full planning, so the project also appears
under Projects.

Roadmap: 1 Measured survey. 2 Class MA prior approval. 3 Pre-application on
the co-living scheme. 4 Full planning application. 5 Technical design.
6 Construction.

### Southgate, Winchester

HMO. Options Tested. October 2025. Only The Decision and the Roadmap are
new. Both the ten-bed and the nine-bed schemes go on the page once the
nine-bed drawing is with you; it is in the Feasibility Examples share under
23 Southgate Street, HD00070 - Proposed Plans.pdf.

**The Proposed Layout, caption.** Option 1 of 2. The ten-bed scheme takes
co-living rooms across the basement, first and second floors above a
partially retained shop, with a sunken garden for the basement room. The
nine-bed scheme trades one room for larger shared space on the same
footprint.

**The Decision.** Two layouts, and one conversation to have before either is
chosen.

The study proved that co-living works in the building at nine or ten rooms
with the shop still trading, and that the difference between the two comes
down to how much shared space the conservation officer and the licensing
team will want to see. Because the building is listed, the next step is not
a planning application but a heritage conversation: the drawings show every
new partition and en suite against the existing fabric, which is exactly
what that conversation needs. The client can brief a heritage consultant and
go to pre-application with a real scheme rather than a hope.

Roadmap: 1 Heritage statement and listed building advice. 2 Pre-application
with Winchester, planning and conservation. 3 Measured survey. 4 Full
planning and listed building consent. 5 Technical design. 6 Construction.

Two corrections to the text while it is open. "IDF" in the construction
materials section is a typo for ICF. And the sentence saying stamp duty
applies "if you build a property worth over £500,000" is wrong; stamp duty
